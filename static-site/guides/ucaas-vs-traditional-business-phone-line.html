UCaaS vs. a traditional business phone line

Updated Nov 2026

UCaaS (unified communications as a service) runs your business phone system in the cloud, so calls, voicemail, video meetings, and team chat work over the internet on desk phones, computers, and mobile apps. A traditional business line (often called POTS) runs over the phone company's copper network. It's simple, but it's limited, and copper lines are being retired in many areas.

For most small businesses with a reliable internet connection, UCaaS is the more flexible choice. Traditional lines still make sense for some single-purpose uses, like an alarm panel, an elevator, or a fax line, and there are POTS replacement services for those.

What to ask before you switch

  1. Is your internet good enough? Voice needs a stable connection with steady upload, not just high download speed. Ask about a backup connection if phones are critical.
  2. Can you keep your numbers? Business numbers can usually be ported. Ask for the timeline and what's needed.
  3. How does 911 work? Federal rules (Kari's Law and RAY BAUM'S Act) require multi-line systems to allow direct 911 dialing and to send a dispatchable location. Make sure each user's location is set correctly.
  4. What happens in an outage? Confirm failover options, such as forwarding to mobile apps or cell numbers.
  5. What else is on your copper lines? Alarm panels, fax, elevators, and point-of-sale terminals may need a POTS replacement service, not a UCaaS seat.
  6. Contract and seats. Term length, number of users, and which features are in each plan.

FAQ

What does UCaaS stand for?

Unified communications as a service: phone, video, messaging, and often contact center tools delivered from the cloud as a subscription.

Is VoIP the same as UCaaS?

VoIP is the technology that carries voice over the internet. UCaaS is a full service built on it, which adds video, chat, mobile apps, and admin tools.

Will my phones work if the internet goes down?

Desk phones on UCaaS need internet. Most systems can forward calls to mobile apps or cell phones, and a backup internet connection reduces the risk.

Can I keep my existing business phone number?

Usually, yes, through number porting. Your provider confirms eligibility and timing.

Do I still need a traditional line for my alarm or fax?

Possibly. Many alarm panels and fax machines were built for copper lines. Ask about a POTS replacement service when you plan the switch.
